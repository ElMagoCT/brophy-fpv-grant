FPV drones built for one war alone

Work

Bridges, power lines, solar farms, roofs and crops are inspected by drone now. It is a multi-billion-dollar industry that hires Part 107 pilots straight out of school.

Culture

FPV shots are in car ads, sports broadcasts and feature films. Racing leagues fill arenas and stream to millions. The skill is visible everywhere; the training is not.

Why it belongs in a school

It is very fun, very skilful, and it takes a lot of learning: physics, electronics, law, judgment. That combination is exactly what a club should be built around.

"The hardest part of FPV is not flying. It is everything you have to understand before you are allowed to." — the club's working motto

What a student walks away able to do

Lowering the barrier to entry

Anyone can start flying today, for free

The sim lab

Five FPV simulators in the IC, open 7:00–4:00 on school days. Tap your name, pick a sim, fly. Crashes cost nothing.

No seat? Ground School

43 lessons on the kiosk or any phone. Drills on the real transmitter; progress follows the pilot.

Zero cost to the student

Sim hours, lessons, quizzes and FAA TRUST are free. Club gear is shared and checked out.

the kiosk · open to anyone

Clear first steps

Name at the kiosk → 7.5 sim hours → Safe Flight and Battery Care quizzes → build and flash a Meteor → first real flight.

Structure that ensures learning

Badges unlock gear

One testable skill

A badge is one specific, testable skill. Earn every badge in a tier and the next drone is yours to fly. "Who may fly what" is written down, not remembered.

Demonstration, not attendance

Quiz badges: 10 questions, 80% passes, the kiosk awards them. Bench badges: checklist, photo, instructor approves. Witnessed badges: a mentor watches you meet the written standard.

The record

One badge catalogue shared by kiosk and website. Every award signed by the approving instructor. Progress only moves forward.

Structure that ensures safety

A written safety standard

The rules

Brophy FPV safe-flight rules, published and quizzed. Passing them is the Safe Flight badge. Nothing over 250 g until the top tier.

Gates before gear

No real drone before Safe Flight and Battery Care. No event flying before FAA TRUST.

Event protocol

A spotter on every flight, a flight zone set up, bystanders briefed, failsafe tested, ducted airframes near people.

Batteries

Balance-charge, storage voltage, puffed-pack check, smoke stopper on every first power-up, fire procedure drilled.

Airspace

B4UFLY check, LAANC when needed under Sky Harbor's Class B, incident report the same day.

the Safe Flight quiz, passed on the kiosk
Structure that ensures sustainability

Built to outlive its founders

Skills stay

Skills normally graduate with the seniors. Here they are badges, and the club is heavily underclassmen, gaining members every week.

Mentors

Coach a new member through two badges, then sign off the badges you hold, with officer approval. The program produces its own teachers every year.

Everything written down

The badge sheet, the safety rules, seven printed posters, and a website with live lab numbers. Kiosk and lessons are built and maintained by students.

Recruiting moments

The fall assembly pitch, a pep-rally race the whole school watches through the goggles, posters above the sims, a public website anyone can open.
