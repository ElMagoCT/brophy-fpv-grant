B. SKILLS WE WANT PEOPLE TO LEARN
====================================================================

B1  Four tracks of skill
    - Flight · Build & tech · Knowledge & safety · Crew & leadership
    - Seven badges in each, 28 total
    - A pilot is not a pilot until they can also build, reason and lead

B2  Hands: build & tech
    - Soldering an ESC and motors, heat-shrink, continuity checks
    - Betaflight: flashing, motor order, binding a radio
    - Field repair: swap a motor or prop, diagnose "won't arm"
    - Electrical components: why a 3-inch and a 5-inch need different parts
    - Transfers straight to robotics and engineering

B3  Judgment: knowledge & safety
    - Battery care: LiPo charging, storage voltage, puffed packs, disposal
    - Airspace: Class B over Phoenix, B4UFLY, LAANC, the 400 ft ceiling
    - Emergency procedures: failsafe, flyaway, LiPo fire, incident report
    - FAA TRUST: the federal baseline, free, for every flying member

B4  Stick skills: flight
    - Simulator hours, orbit, Split-S, gaps, finishing a race
    - Line of sight: hover and land without goggles
    - Tiny whoop → freestyle → indoor proximity → racing
    - Every flight skill is tested in the sim before it costs a battery

B5  Working with people: crew & leadership
    - Spotter: the pilot can't see around themselves
    - Shot planning with a coach or moderator
    - Event ops: flight zones, briefing bystanders, call-outs
    - Mentor, Examiner, Fleet Steward: running the program itself

B6  Media skills
    - Cinematography: follow, orbit, reveal, deliver one usable clip
    - Video systems: binding O4, resolution, D-log vs normal, ND filters
    - Editing: cut and grade a 30–60 s clip
    - Tuning: fix an oscillation with before/after footage

B7  What a graduate can do
    - Build and repair a drone from parts
    - Fly a camera drone at a school event with a spotter, legally
    - Teach a new member and sign off their badges
    - Hand a coach a finished clip

C. LOWERING THE BARRIER TO ENTRY
====================================================================

C1  The sim lab: fly today, for free
    - Five FPV simulators in the IC, open 7:00–4:00 on school days
    - Open to anyone: tap your name, pick a sim, fly
    - Crashes cost nothing; nobody needs to own anything
    - Live numbers: hours flown, pilots logged, flying this week

C2  No seat? Ground School
    - 45 lessons, about 140 minutes, on the kiosk or any phone
    - Lessons, drills on the real transmitter, quizzes
    - Progress follows the pilot between kiosk and website
    - A new member has something to do on day one

C3  Zero cost to the student
    - Sim hours, lessons, quizzes, FAA TRUST: all free
    - Club gear is shared and checked out; no personal drone needed
    - Study materials and mentoring are run by students

C4  Clear first steps
    - Add your name at the kiosk → Simulator Flight (7.5 h)
    - Safe Flight and Battery Care quizzes
    - Build and flash a Meteor at the bench
    - Then your first real flight: the gate opens

C5  Recruiting moments
    - Fall assembly pitch alongside recruitment
    - A pep-rally race where the whole school sees FPV through the goggles
    - Posters above the sims and a public website anyone can open

D. STRUCTURE THAT ENSURES LEARNING
====================================================================

D1  Badges unlock gear
    - A badge is one specific, testable skill
    - Earn every badge in a tier and the next drone is yours to fly
    - Four tiers: Simulator → Tiny Whoop → Full-size → Event Pilot
    - "Who may fly what" is written down, not remembered

D2  Demonstration, not attendance
    - Quiz badges: 10 questions, 80% passes, the kiosk awards them
    - Bench badges: checklist on the kiosk, photo of the work, instructor approves
    - Witnessed badges: an examiner watches you meet the written standard
    - Official: FAA TRUST certificate on file

D3  The tier ladder (visual)
    - Tier 0 Simulator: 7 badges → Gate 1
    - Tier 1 Tiny Whoop (Meteor 75 Pro): 6 badges → Gate 2
    - Tier 2 Full-size (Pavo 20 Pro, Cinebot 35, 5-inch): 8 badges → Gate 3
    - Tier 3 Event Pilot: fly for a crowd, with a spotter
    - Seven electives on the side

D4  Every badge has a "why"
    - Battery Care because LiPos are the real fire risk
    - Airspace because Brophy is near Sky Harbor
    - Spotter because FPV pilots can't see around themselves
    - Mentor because the program must train its replacements

D5  The record
    - One badge catalogue shared by kiosk and website
    - Every award signed by the approving instructor
    - Progress only moves forward; a badge can't be lost in a drawer
    - A visible board that says exactly what each member can do

D6  Why sim first
    - 7.5 hours before the first real flight
    - Proficient Flight is sim-only on purpose: it gates the first battery
    - Line of Sight: land it when the video dies
    - Each gate sits where the next mistake starts to cost money or safety

====================================================================
E. STRUCTURE THAT ENSURES SAFETY
====================================================================

E1  A written safety standard
    - Brophy FPV safe-flight rules, published and quizzed
    - Nothing over 250 g until the top tier
    - No real drone before Safe Flight, Battery Care and FAA TRUST

E2  Flying near people
    - Event Pilot requires Spotter, Event Ops, Cinematography, TRUST
    - Flight zone set up and bystanders briefed before every event flight
    - Ducted cinewhoops for anything near a crowd

E3  Batteries and fire
    - Balance charging, storage voltage, puffed-pack checks, disposal
    - Smoke stopper on every first power-up
    - LiPo fire procedure drilled, not read

E4  Airspace and the law
    - Phoenix Sky Harbor Class B: check B4UFLY, request LAANC when needed
    - FAA TRUST for recreational flyers; Part 107 study group for those who want more
    - Incident reporting written the same day

E5  Who may fly what, who may sign off
    - Tiers answer "who may fly what"
    - Examiner badge answers "who may sign off"
    - Officer approval on top; Fleet Steward tracks the gear
    - Open item: damage and liability policy

====================================================================
F. STRUCTURE THAT ENSURES SUSTAINABILITY
====================================================================

F1  Built to outlive its founders
    - Skills normally graduate with the seniors; here they're badges
    - Mentor: coach a new member through two badges
    - Examiner: defined by badges held plus officer approval
    - The program produces its own teachers every year

F2  Everything is written down
    - Badge sheet, safety rules, seven printed posters
    - A website that runs itself, with live lab numbers
    - Kiosk and lessons already built and maintained by students

F3  Gear bought to last
    - Goggles and radios about 8 years; airframes about 4
    - Spares, frames and props in the budget: crashes are repairs, not purchases
    - One-time build-out, not a recurring ask

F4  Accountability for school gear
    - Fleet Steward runs check-in/out and a maintenance log each semester
    - Where the gear lives, who holds the key
    - Officer roles: VP, Secretary, Equipment Manager, Safety Officer

F5  A model other clubs can copy
    - Badge → gate → gear is not drone-specific
    - Robotics, shop, A/V: any club with expensive tools and a safety question
    - The documents and website are reusable
