Slide 3 — The problem

Label: 02 The problem

Headline: Every year a club like this restarts from zero.

Drones. They get flown by the two people who already knew how, and when those two graduate the drones sit in a drawer.

A training fleet for a certification system. The drones in the budget are the hardware the badge program runs on, and the program is what the school keeps.

  • Skills leave with the seniors. Whoever knew how to build, tune and fly graduates, and nothing is written down.
  • Nobody can say who may fly what. Expensive gear with no rule for who is trained to touch it.
  • FPV looks like a toy until it isn't. LiPo fires, flyaways and controlled airspace are real, and there is no safety standard on paper.
  • Students who want to film for the school have no path to get there.

Slide 6 — How a badge is earned

Label: 05 How a badge is earned

Headline: Demonstration, not attendance.

  • Quiz badges: short lessons, then a 10-question quiz. 80% passes, retake any time, the kiosk awards it by itself.
  • Bench badges: a step-by-step checklist on the kiosk, a photo of the work, an instructor approves.
  • Witnessed badges: an examiner watches you perform the standard written on the card.
  • Official: the free FAA TRUST certificate before anyone touches a real drone.
  • — why: students run every sign-off. Nothing here costs the school anything beyond the training fleet.

Speaker notes

How does a badge actually get earned? Three ways, plus one official one.

▶ Click: quiz badges

Knowledge badges are quizzes. Short lessons, then ten questions drawn from that badge's pool. Eighty percent passes. You can retake as often as you like; your best score counts. The kiosk awards those by itself.

▶ Click: bench badges

Bench badges are hands-on: a step-by-step checklist on the kiosk or on your phone. You tick the boxes, DM a photo of the work, and an instructor approves it.

▶ Click: witnessed badges

Witnessed badges are the flying ones. An examiner who already holds the badge watches you do it against the standard printed on the card. For Tiny Whoop that's take-off, a figure-eight through two gates, an orbit, and a landing on the pad with no wall contact.

▶ Click: FAA TRUST

And before anyone touches a real drone, the free FAA TRUST certificate. Twenty minutes, can't be failed, zero cost to the school or the student.

▶ Click: students run every sign-off

The point to hold onto: students run all of it. The sign-offs, the study material, the mentoring. The only thing this program needs from the school is the training fleet.

Slide 8 — Why sim first

Label: 07 Why sim first

Headline: Crashes should cost nothing while you learn.

  • 7.5 hours in the simulator before the first real flight. Acro muscle memory with zero risk.
  • Proficient Flight is sim-only on purpose: orbit, Split-S, gaps, a finished race. It gates the first real battery.
  • Line of Sight: if the video cuts out, the pilot can still level out and land.
  • Then a sub-250 g whoop that can't do much damage. Full-size only after Freestyle, Electrical Components, Radio Protocol and Field Repair.
  • — why: every gate is placed where the next mistake starts to cost real money or real safety.

[Photo coming from the kiosk: A pilot mid-flight in Liftoff on the kiosk — assets/img/kiosk/kiosk-liftoff.jpg]

Speaker notes

Why do we make people fly a simulator for seven and a half hours first?

▶ Click: 7.5 hours

Because crashes should cost nothing while you're learning. In the sim you can crash a hundred times a night and the only thing you lose is pride.

▶ Click: Proficient Flight is sim-only

Proficient Flight is sim-only on purpose. Orbit, Split-S, gaps, finish a race. It's the badge that gates the first real battery.

▶ Click: Line of Sight

Line of Sight is the one people skip in other clubs: without goggles, take off, hover and land in self-levelling mode. Because if your video feed cuts out, you need to be able to look up and land the drone.

▶ Click: then a sub-250 g whoop

Then your first real aircraft is a tiny whoop that can't do much damage. Full-size only after Freestyle, Electrical Components, Radio Protocol and Field Repair.

▶ Click: every gate is placed where mistakes start to cost

Every gate sits exactly where the next mistake starts to cost real money or real safety.
