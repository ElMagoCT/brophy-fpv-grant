Slide 3 — Track: Flight

Label: Skills · track 1 of 4

Headline: Flight

Speaker notes

No bullets to click; these seven cards are the whole track.

The Flight track takes someone from never having flown to racing. Simulator Flight is seven and a half hours in the sim. Proficient Flight is orbit, Split-S, gaps and a finished race, in the sim, so it can gate the first real battery. Line of Sight is landing without goggles, for the day the video cuts out.

Then real aircraft: Tiny Whoop on a sub-250-gram drone, Freestyle, Indoor Proximity through a doorway course, and Racing: three clean laps under a target time.

Slide 5 — Track: Knowledge &amp; safety

Label: Skills · track 3 of 4

Headline: Knowledge & safety

Speaker notes

This is the track that makes the program responsible rather than just fun.

Safe Flight is the club's written rules, quizzed. Electrical Components and Radio Protocol are the theory behind why parts match and why video drops out. Airspace matters because Brophy sits near Sky Harbor's controlled airspace. Emergency Procedures is a plan for when something goes wrong. Event Ops is the ground work before flying at a game. And FAA TRUST is the federal certificate: free, twenty minutes, required of every flying member.

Slide 9 — Structure that ensures learning

Label: Structure that ensures learning

Headline: Badges unlock gear

  • A badge is one specific, testable skill. Earn every badge in a tier and the next drone is yours to fly. "Who may fly what" is written down, not remembered.
  • Demonstration, not attendance. Quiz badges: 10 questions, 80% passes, the kiosk awards them. Bench badges: checklist, photo of the work, instructor approves. Witnessed badges: an examiner watches you meet the written standard. FAA TRUST on file.
  • The record. One badge catalogue shared by kiosk and website. Every award signed by the approving instructor. Progress only moves forward. A visible board says exactly what each member can do.

Speaker notes

Here's the structure underneath it.

▶ Click: badges unlock gear

A badge is one specific, testable skill. Earn every badge in a tier and the next drone is yours to fly. So "who may fly what" is written down, not remembered.

▶ Click: demonstration, not attendance

Badges are earned by doing, not by showing up. Knowledge badges are ten-question quizzes the kiosk grades and awards itself. Bench badges are a checklist and a photo of the work, approved by an instructor. Flying badges are witnessed by an examiner against a written standard. And the FAA certificate goes on file.

Slide 10 — Structure that ensures safety

Label: Structure that ensures safety

Headline: A written safety standard

  • Brophy FPV safe-flight rules, published and quizzed. Passing them is the Safe Flight badge.
  • Nothing over 250 g until the top tier.
  • No real drone before Safe Flight and Battery Care. No event flying before FAA TRUST.
  • Event protocol: a spotter on every flight, a flight zone set up, bystanders briefed, failsafe tested, ducted airframes near people.
  • Batteries: balance-charge, storage voltage, puffed-pack check, smoke stopper on every first power-up, fire procedure drilled.
  • Airspace: B4UFLY check, LAANC when needed under Sky Harbor's Class B, incident report the same day.
